          Rules for changing a rule

          • A cell replaces the default only when it beats control on net value per eligible customer, at the planned sample size, with guardrails intact (opt-out rate, complaints, 90-day delinquency, overall product engagement).
          • Results are read by segment. A cap that is right for landscapers may be wrong for design studios.
          • Any campaign whose unsubscribe rate passes 0.3% is throttled automatically while it’s reviewed.
          • A small share of traffic keeps exploring other values, so the engine notices when behavior shifts.

                How a decision is made

                1. Gate: eligibility, suppressions (risk, snooze), consent, weekly cap of 4 touches.
                2. Candidates: every consented channel at every allowed hour in the next 4 days, plus “wait”.
                3. Score: timed benefit minus cadence, fatigue and opt-out costs.
                4. Pick: the highest net option; wait when nothing clears $8.
                benefit = value × 6% × affinity × (0.5 + intent/100) × timing × calendar × 0.5^(hours until send ÷ 120) timing = 0.6 + 0.4 × engagement rhythm at that hour and day calendar = 1.15 when it lands 6–48 h before payroll or a bill cadence = benefit × (1 − hours since customer action ÷ 24) × 0.8 (× 0.25 in-product; 0 after 24 hours) fatigue = $4 × touches in the last 7 days repeat = 20% of benefit if this channel just went unanswered opt-out = P(opt-out) × 12-month value of the channel P(opt-out) = base × (1 + 0.5 × unanswered on channel + 0.25 × touches this week) net = benefit − cadence − fatigue − repeat − opt-out intent = fades to baseline with a 5-day half-life

                Timing, cadence and snoozes

                • Time of day and day of week: each customer has an engagement rhythm, starting from their segment and refined by their own logins and clicks.
                • Business calendar: touches 6–48 hours before payroll or a large bill get a timing lift, because that is when cash planning happens.
                • At least 20 hours between marketing touches; 48 hours between emails (96 after “fewer emails”); 72 hours between texts.
                • Abandoned application: one message right away and one reminder from any channel within 24 hours, with no cadence cost.
                • “Not now” reasons: don’t need funding → 30 days; remind me later → 7 days; worried about cost → 7 days, then a cost message; already have financing → 30 days. A new need signal ends any snooze early.
                • Three touches in a row with no response: pause until a new need signal.

                Protecting the channel

                • An unsubscribe, a STOP or notifications turned off is the worst outcome: the channel is gone for every future offer. The opt-out cost puts a price on that risk before every send.
                • Risk rises with unanswered touches and frequency, so after a few ignores an extra email can be worth less than nothing.
                • The preference center offers “fewer emails” first, so customers can turn the volume down instead of off.
                • Opt-outs are per channel: an email unsubscribe doesn’t end in-product messages.
                • Late payment: all promotion stops and the customer moves to Servicing.

                What this demo is and isn’t

                • Synthetic customers and illustrative numbers. Scoring is a transparent rule set; in production, intent, affinity, timing and opt-out risk come from Data Science models trained on real behavior.
                • Eligibility, offer amounts and pricing come from Risk. Messages come from a library approved by Legal and Compliance.
                • Live AI only narrates decisions and drafts copy. Decisions and the compliance check are deterministic.
